2 - Overlay Design Pattern Research

January 29th, 2026

Prior to design, modals/overlays weren’t something I ever thought about. Naema Baskanderi mentions users clicking away because they’re used to modals being popups that they just want to get rid of. After reading the article, I’ve learned that modals can be useful and that I’ve already seen plenty of usable modals in my life.

One of the purposes of a modal is to catch the attention of the user while still maintaining the context of the page they are currently on. Instead of going to a new page, the content on the page is “pushed back” or out of focus, allowing the user to focus on whatever the designer/website wants them to see or do.

Like many other design patterns, it's important to keep accessibility in mind for the user. Not only does it allow navigation to more people, it also makes the process less irritating. General rules like making buttons and titles descriptive of course help the user understand what the modal is for, but specific practices can make them better.

The size of the modal shouldn’t take up more than half the page, so the user can still see which page they’re on. This is also why using modals on mobile is not recommended, as mobile screens are often too small. Another good practice to have with modals is giving it a proper focus. The background of the original page being visible is important for context, but could distract from the overlay, which defeats the purpose of it. Darkening it or using a “lightbox effect” is a common way of bringing the focus to the modal while still showing the page.
